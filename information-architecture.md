# Global UX Research Portfolio - IA & Page Structure Specification

## Information Architecture Overview

```
Homepage
├── Case Studies (Collection)
│   ├── Case Study 1 (Individual)
│   ├── Case Study 2 (Individual)
│   ├── Case Study N (Individual)
├── About
├── Research Process
├── Community & Speaking
├── Resume
└── Contact
```

---

## Page Specifications

### 1. HOMEPAGE

**Purpose:** Communicate positioning and drive exploration into detailed content.

**Primary User Goals:**
- Understand who the researcher is
- Determine if their specialization is relevant
- Build initial trust
- Know what action to take next

**Key Requirements:**

#### Hero Section
Must immediately answer:
- Who are you?
- What do you specialize in?
- Why should visitors trust you?
- What should visitors do next?

**Content Elements:**
- Strong headline (clear positioning statement)
- Subheading (brief specialization summary)
- Primary visual or supporting image
- Primary CTA (e.g., "View Case Studies")
- Secondary CTA (e.g., "Download Resume" or "Get in Touch")

**Layout Consideration:**
- Simple, uncluttered design
- Maximum 2-3 key messages
- Mobile-first responsiveness
- Strong visual hierarchy

---

#### Credibility Section
**Purpose:** Establish authority and trust signals.

**Content Elements:**
- Years of experience
- Industries worked in
- Organizations/companies contributed to
- Community contributions
- Open-source work
- Awards or recognition (if applicable)
- Key statistics (e.g., "Influenced 5+ product decisions")

**Presentation Options:**
- Timeline of experience
- Logo grid of organizations
- Badge/achievement system
- Testimonial section
- Statistics/metrics

**Layout Consideration:**
- Scannable format (not paragraph-heavy)
- Visual hierarchy that draws attention
- Mobile-optimized display

---

#### Featured Case Studies Section
**Purpose:** Showcase high-impact work and drive deeper engagement.

**Content Elements:**
- 2-4 featured case studies (highest impact projects first)
- Each case study should display:
  - Project title
  - Brief context (1-2 sentences)
  - Key outcome or metric
  - Visual thumbnail or representative image
  - Link to full case study

**Requirements:**
- Outcome-focused summaries (what changed, not what was done)
- Clear visual distinction from other content
- Easy entry point to detailed case studies
- Mobile-optimized cards or list format

**Layout Consideration:**
- Card-based layout or featured list
- Consistent spacing and hierarchy
- Prominent CTAs to individual case studies

---

#### Value Proposition / Specialization Section
**Purpose:** Clearly articulate what the researcher specializes in.

**Content Elements:**
- 3-5 key specializations or research methods
- Why each matters
- Who benefits from this expertise

**Presentation Options:**
- Icon-text pairs
- Short cards with descriptions
- Tag/pill-based system
- Category breakdown

**Layout Consideration:**
- Quick scannable format
- Use of white space
- Visual consistency

---

#### CTA Section (Before Footer)
**Purpose:** Final conversion opportunity.

**Content Elements:**
- Primary CTA: "View All Case Studies"
- Secondary CTA: "Download Resume" or "Schedule a Conversation"
- Optional: Email signup for updates
- Contact information (email or contact form link)

**Layout Consideration:**
- High visual prominence
- Clear contrast
- Mobile-friendly button sizing

---

**Conversion Requirements for Homepage:**
- Resume accessible within one click
- Contact options always visible
- CTAs appear multiple times throughout
- No more than 2 clicks to reach detailed case studies

---

### 2. CASE STUDIES (Collection Page)

**Purpose:** Overview of all research work with filtering/browsing capability.

**Primary User Goals:**
- Browse available case studies
- Filter by industry, method, or outcome type
- Quick assess quality of work

**Content Elements:**
- Introduction/header explaining the collection
- Full list of case studies with:
  - Title
  - Brief description (1-2 sentences)
  - Outcome metric or impact statement
  - Visual thumbnail
  - Link to full case study

**Optional Features:**
- Filtering by industry, research method, or project type
- Sorting by date, impact, or relevance
- Search functionality
- Categorization system

**Layout Consideration:**
- Card-based or list-based layout
- Consistent thumbnail sizing
- Mobile-optimized grid
- Clear hierarchy

**Conversion Requirements:**
- Each case study easily accessible with one click
- CTAs to individual case studies obvious

---

### 3. CASE STUDY (Individual Page)

**Purpose:** Demonstrate research rigor, methodology, strategic thinking, and impact.

**Primary User Goals:**
- Understand the problem and context
- Validate research methodology
- See evidence of impact
- Assess quality of thinking and communication

**Required Content Structure:**

**3.1 Overview Section**
- Project title
- Brief summary (1-2 sentences)
- Key metric or business outcome
- Timeframe/duration

**3.2 Context Section**
- Business/product context
- Industry or domain
- Company/organization background (if applicable)
- Market conditions or competitive landscape

**3.3 Problem Statement**
- What was the core problem?
- Why did it matter?
- Who was affected?

**3.4 Research Objectives**
- What specific questions needed answers?
- What decisions would be informed?
- What success looked like

**3.5 Role & Team**
- Your specific role
- Team composition
- Stakeholder involvement
- Leadership or collaboration involvement

**3.6 Constraints**
- Timeline constraints
- Budget or resource constraints
- Organizational constraints
- Technical or logistical constraints

**3.7 Research Methodology**
- Research methods used (e.g., user interviews, surveys, usability testing, analytics analysis)
- Why these methods were chosen
- How they addressed the research objectives

**3.8 Research Process / Activities**
- Detailed breakdown of research activities
- Recruitment approach and sample size
- Data collection process
- Analysis approach
- Timeline of activities

**3.9 Key Findings**
- 3-7 major findings from the research
- Evidence supporting each finding
- Data or quotes (if applicable)
- Visual representations (charts, screenshots, etc.)

**3.10 Insights**
- What the findings meant
- Deeper interpretation beyond raw data
- Strategic implications
- How findings addressed research objectives

**3.11 Recommendations**
- Specific, actionable recommendations based on research
- How recommendations addressed the problem
- Prioritization of recommendations (if applicable)

**3.12 Business Impact / Product Decisions**
- Which decisions were made based on the research
- How the research influenced product strategy
- Metrics improved or outcomes achieved
- Long-term impact

**3.13 Reflection & Lessons Learned**
- What you learned through the process
- What you would do differently
- How this project shaped your approach to research
- Open questions or future directions

---

**Content Quality Requirements:**
- Focus on *why* things were done, not just *what* was done
- Emphasize decision-making influence
- Include specific outcomes and metrics
- Balance strategic insight with methodological rigor
- Accessible language (avoid overly academic terminology)
- Clear visual hierarchy within the case study

**Layout Consideration:**
- Scannable sections with clear headings
- Generous whitespace
- Mixed content types (text, data visualizations, screenshots, quotes)
- Mobile-responsive layout
- Consistent typography and spacing

**Conversion Requirements:**
- Clear navigation to other case studies
- Resume download accessible
- Contact CTA at end of case study
- Social sharing options (optional)

**Primary User Goals:**
- Understand the researcher's background and values
- Build personal connection
- Assess cultural fit or collaboration style
- Determine if they share similar interests or values

**Content Elements:**

**4.1 Personal Introduction**
- Brief professional background
- Career journey narrative
- Why research matters to them
- Personal mission or philosophy

**4.2 Expertise Areas**
- Core competencies
- Research specializations
- Industries or domains of expertise
- Unique perspective or approach

**4.3 Values & Approach**
- How they work with teams
- Commitment to accessibility or inclusive design
- Research philosophy
- What they believe makes great research

**4.4 Background Story**
- How they got into UX research
- Key turning points or experiences
- What drives their work

**4.5 Personal Interests**
- Hobbies or interests outside work
- Community involvement
- Open-source contributions
- Writing or speaking topics

**4.6 Contact & Availability**
- How to reach them
- Types of opportunities they're interested in
- Response time expectations

**4.7 Links & Resources**
- LinkedIn profile
- Twitter/X or other social accounts
- GitHub or portfolio links
- Newsletter or blog (if applicable)

---

**Content Quality Requirements:**
- Authentic and personal, but professionally appropriate
- Conversational tone
- Memorable details
- Clear values and motivations
- Accessible language

**Layout Consideration:**
- Mix of text and visual elements
- Personal photo or avatar
- Timeline or narrative structure
- Mobile-optimized

### 5. RESEARCH PROCESS

**Purpose:** Explain methodology and working style to build credibility and trust.

**Primary User Goals:**
- Understand the researcher's approach to research
- Assess methodological rigor
- Determine fit for specific project types
- Build confidence in their process

**Content Elements:**

**5.1 Research Philosophy**
- Core beliefs about research
- Why this approach matters
- How it leads to better outcomes

**5.2 Research Process Overview**
- High-level framework or stages
- Visual representation (flowchart or diagram)
- How different research methods fit into the process

**5.3 Key Research Methods**
- Methods they specialize in (e.g., user interviews, surveys, usability testing, ethnographic research, analytics analysis)
- When and why each method is appropriate
- Strengths and limitations of each
- Examples of application

**5.4 Stakeholder Engagement**
- How they involve stakeholders in the research
- How they communicate findings
- How they ensure findings drive action

**5.5 Data Analysis Approach**
- How they analyze qualitative data
- How they handle quantitative data
- How they synthesize across data types
- How they identify patterns and insights

**5.6 Accessibility & Inclusive Research**
- Commitment to accessibility in research design
- How they ensure diverse participant recruitment
- Accessibility considerations in data collection
- How findings account for diverse user needs

**5.7 Timeline & Typical Engagement Model**
- Typical project timeline
- Flexibility for different project types
- How they work with teams

---

**Content Quality Requirements:**
- Educational without being overly academic
- Clear connection between process and outcomes
- Use of examples or case study references
- Accessibility-first thinking evident
- Actionable insights

**Layout Consideration:**
- Visual diagrams or flowcharts
- Section-based structure
- Mixed content types
- Mobile-responsive

- Mentoring activities

- Category-based grouping

### 8. CONTACT

**Purpose:** Reduce friction for outreach and provide multiple contact methods.

**Primary User Goals:**
- Initiate contact
- Schedule a conversation
- Send a message

**Content Elements:**

**8.1 Contact Information**
- Email address
- Phone number (optional)
- Location

**8.2 Contact Form**
- Name field
- Email field
- Subject/opportunity type (dropdown: Hiring, Freelance, Speaking, Collaboration, Other)
- Message field
- Submit button
- Confirmation message

**8.3 Alternative Contact Methods**
- Links to LinkedIn
- Links to Twitter/X or other social platforms
- Links to email (mailto: link)
- Calendar link for scheduling (if using Calendly, Calendoola, or similar)

**8.4 Response Expectations**
- Expected response time
- What to expect in the response
- Best times to reach out

**8.5 Optional: FAQ or Common Questions**
- How to best reach them
- Types of opportunities they're interested in
- Collaboration models
- Typical project timelines

---

**Content Quality Requirements:**
- Clear and welcoming tone
- Multiple contact options
- Quick response expectations set
- Accessibility-compliant form

**Layout Consideration:**
- Centered, simple layout
- Form fields clearly labeled
- Mobile-friendly form
- Optional: embedded calendar widget
- Whitespace around contact elements

**Conversion Requirements:**
- Contact form functional and tested
- Multiple contact options available
- Clear feedback after form submission
- No more than 3 fields required for initial contact

## Cross-Page Navigation Requirements

**Header/Navigation:**
- Visible on all pages
- Includes links to: Home, Case Studies, About, Research Process, Community & Speaking, Resume, Contact
- Mobile hamburger menu (if needed)
- Clear current page indicator
- Responsive and sticky (optional)

**Footer:**
- Links to main pages
- Contact information or email signup
- Social media links
- Copyright information
- Optional: newsletter signup

**Internal Navigation:**
- Previous/next case study links on individual case study pages
- Related case studies suggestions
- Clear back navigation
- Breadcrumb navigation (optional)

---

## Mobile Responsiveness Requirements

- All pages fully responsive at viewport widths 320px and above
- Touch-friendly button sizing (minimum 44x44px)
- Readable typography at all sizes
- Optimized image loading
- Mobile-first navigation (hamburger menu or collapsed nav)
- Form fields optimized for mobile input
- Case study content reflow appropriately

---

## Accessibility Requirements (WCAG 2.1 AA)

- Color contrast ratios: minimum 4.5:1 for normal text, 3:1 for large text
- Keyboard navigation support throughout
- Semantic HTML structure
- Alt text for all images
- Form labels associated with inputs
- Focus states clearly visible
- Screen reader compatibility
- Captions or transcripts for any video content
- Clear heading hierarchy (h1, h2, h3, etc.)
- Sufficient color differentiation (not relying on color alone)
